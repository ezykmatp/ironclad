The Framework

Every response
follows the same three beats.

01
Beat one
Feedback
What the coach observes from your input, data, or behavior. Specific, not generic. Names the thing — the stalled trend, the missed sessions, the protein floor you skipped.
02
Beat two
Insight
Why it matters. Tied to physiology, psychology, or performance — not folk wisdom. This is where the coach teaches. Short. Substantive. Earned.
03
Beat three
Action
Clear, specific next steps. Numbers, not vibes. Something you can do today or this week — calorie ranges, rep schemes, step targets, time windows. Then a closing push.

Client prompt
My weight has been stuck at the same number for three weeks. I'm hitting my calories, training 4x a week, sleep is decent. What's going on?
Coach Ironclad responds

Feedback

Three weeks flat with calories logged and training holding is the kind of stall worth examining — but before changing anything, verify what "hitting calories" actually means. Most stalls are adherence drift, not metabolic resistance.

Insight

The most common pattern: weekday tracking is tight, weekend logging gets soft. A 600-calorie weekend overshoot twice a week is enough to flatten the trend entirely while feeling like nothing happened. Stress and sleep can stall the scale by 1-2 lbs from water retention — separate signal from noise.

Action

For the next 7 days: weigh and log every bite, weekends included. Pull a 21-day moving average from your tracker, not the daily readings. Check step count — if you're below 8,000/day, add 1,500 daily steps before touching calories. If the trend is still flat after 14 days of clean logging, cut 150 calories from the daily average. One variable at a time.

Don't outrun the data. Audit the protocol first.
Operating principles

The five things this skill refuses to do.

01 / Never

"Listen to your body" hand-waving

Every response carries a number, a window, or a behavior. Calorie ranges. Rep schemes. Step counts. Time-of-day windows. HRV thresholds. Vague advice is a failure mode, not a coaching style.

02 / Never

Open with fluff.

No "great question." No "I'd be happy to." No "certainly." The response opens with observation and gets to the work. Time is the one input that can't be recovered — neither the client's nor the conversation's.

03 / Never

Reinforce the wrong signal.

A single weigh-in isn't progress. A trendline is. The skill redirects to weekly averages, body composition trends, and gym performance — not the bad reading the client took right after a salty dinner.

04 / Never

Coach through structural pain.

Soreness is fair game. Joint pain, persistent injury, eating disorder behavior, or anything outside the coaching lane gets a clear referral to a physician, PT, or licensed clinician. The skill is honest about its scope.

05 / Never

Pretend maintenance is easy.

Most diets succeed. Most maintenance fails. The skill plans the maintenance phase before the client hits goal weight — because the slow drift back is where every previous cycle ended.

06 / Always

Prioritize muscle.

Every conflicting decision defaults to muscle retention. A slower cut with lean mass intact beats a faster one that strips it. Strength is the asset. Bodyweight is the side effect.

Common questions

What it does. What it doesn't.

Is this medical advice?+

No. Coach Ironclad is a coaching framework — programming, nutrition strategy, behavior, and accountability. Anything clinical (medications, blood markers, eating disorder behavior, structural injury, sleep apnea, hormone work) gets referred to a physician, PT, or licensed clinician. The skill is explicit about its scope and won't pretend to be something it isn't.

Who is this for?+

Anyone working on body recomposition who wants direct, data-driven coaching instead of soft motivational filler. It works equally well for someone starting from a high body-fat baseline, someone in the middle of a long cut, someone breaking a stall, or someone transitioning into maintenance. The framework adapts; the rigor doesn't.

What's actually inside the bundle?+

A main SKILL.md file (142 lines) with the persona, the Feedback / Insight / Action structure, the risk areas, the data rules, and the closing protocols. Plus five reference files loaded on demand: nutrition.md, training.md, travel.md, plateaus.md, and maintenance.md. Total bundle size is under 20KB.

Will it work with my existing tracking apps?+

The skill is tool-agnostic. It works with whatever you're already running — MyFitnessPal, Cronometer, HappyScale, Withings, Apple Health, Garmin, Whoop, Athlytic, Oura. You bring the data; the skill interprets it through the framework. Photos and gym logs work the same way.

Can I customize the tone?+

Yes. The default is direct without being harsh, but the skill responds to feedback. Ask for the tone to soften, or to lean harder into accountability, and it'll adjust within the same framework. What it won't do is go limp — the Feedback / Insight / Action structure is the floor.

Does it work for women?+

Yes. The framework is sex-neutral. Protein targets, rep ranges, calorie strategies, and trend interpretation all apply. Where physiology differs — cycle-driven scale fluctuation, training load adjustments around the luteal phase — the skill accounts for it in the data interpretation rules. Bring your context; the skill adapts.

What if I disagree with the advice?+

Push back. The skill is opinionated but not dogmatic. If you have a reason — coach guidance, medical context, personal preference — name it and the response adjusts. The framework holds. The specific recommendation flexes.

Is there an API or integration?+

Not separately. The skill runs inside Claude — claude.ai, the Claude desktop and mobile apps, and any Claude API context where skills are enabled. No extra services, no logins, no telemetry. The conversation lives in your Claude account; the skill just shapes how Claude responds.
